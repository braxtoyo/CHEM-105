CATEGORY 9: ATOMS, IONS, AND CHARGES

SUBATOMIC PARTICLES
Proton: positive charge; nucleus; approximately 1 amu.
Neutron: no charge; nucleus; approximately 1 amu.
Electron: negative charge; outside nucleus; much smaller mass, approximately 1/1836 of proton mass.
Most atomic mass is in the nucleus.

PARTICLE COUNTS
Atomic number Z = number of protons.
The proton count determines the element.
Mass number A = protons plus neutrons.
Neutrons = A−Z.
Neutral atom: electrons equal protons.
Positive ion: fewer electrons than protons.
Negative ion: more electrons than protons.
Signed charge = protons minus electrons.

EXAMPLES
Ca has 20 protons. Ca2+ has 18 electrons.
Cl has 17 protons. Cl− has 18 electrons.
Changing electrons changes charge, not element identity.
Changing protons changes the element.
Changing neutrons changes the isotope.

ISOTOPES VERSUS IONS
Isotopes of one element have the same protons and different neutrons.
Ions have a net charge because proton and electron counts differ.
A species is not an ion merely because it appears in an isoelectronic list.
Neutral Ar has no net charge.

ELECTROSTATIC INTERACTIONS
Like charges repel.
Opposite charges attract.
Larger charge magnitudes increase force magnitude.
Greater separation decreases force magnitude.
Coulomb force follows an inverse-square distance relationship.
