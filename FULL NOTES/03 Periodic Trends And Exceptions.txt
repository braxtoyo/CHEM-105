CATEGORY 3: PERIODIC TRENDS AND EXCEPTIONS

IONIZATION EXCEPTIONS
B has lower first ionization energy than Be. Al has lower first ionization energy than Mg.
Reason: removing the first p electron is easier than removing an electron from the filled s subshell.
O has lower first ionization energy than N. S has lower first ionization energy than P.
Reason: paired-electron repulsion makes an electron easier to remove than from the half-filled p arrangement.

ELECTRON-AFFINITY EXCEPTIONS
Electron addition generally becomes more favorable toward the upper right, but this has important exceptions.
Halogens have strongly negative electron affinities.
Noble gases have unfavorable electron addition.
Group 2 atoms have filled s subshells, making addition relatively unfavorable.
Group 15 atoms have half-filled p subshells, making addition less favorable than a simple trend predicts.
Chlorine has a more negative electron affinity than fluorine.
Fluorine’s small orbital causes greater electron-electron repulsion.
A single periodic-table arrow cannot correctly rank every electron-affinity comparison.

EFFECTIVE NUCLEAR CHARGE
Zeff is the effective positive nuclear charge felt by an electron.
The nucleus attracts electrons. Other electrons provide shielding.
A simple estimate subtracts shielding from nuclear charge. Using the number of core electrons as shielding is an approximation.
Across a period, Zeff generally increases.
Core electrons shield more effectively than electrons in the same shell.
Stronger effective attraction tends to pull electrons closer and make removal harder.

ATOMIC SIZE
Atomic radius generally increases down a group because additional shells are occupied.
Atomic radius generally decreases from left to right because effective nuclear attraction increases.
Largest atoms are generally toward the bottom left.
Do not assume a simple trend resolves every comparison between unrelated positions.

ION SIZE
Cations are smaller than their neutral parent atoms.
Anions are larger than their neutral parent atoms.
Losing an entire outer shell can produce a large size decrease.
Adding electrons increases electron-electron repulsion.

ISOELECTRONIC SERIES
Isoelectronic species have the same electron count.
Within an isoelectronic series: More protons means stronger attraction and smaller radius.
Largest to smallest: O2−, F−, Ne, Na+, Mg2+.
All five have 10 electrons. Their proton counts increase from 8 to 12.

IONIZATION ENERGY
Ionization energy is the energy needed to remove an electron from a gaseous atom or ion.
Removal requires energy. Ionization energy is positive and endothermic.
It generally increases up and to the right.
It generally decreases down and to the left.

SUCCESSIVE IONIZATION
Successive ionization energies increase.
After all valence electrons are removed, the next removal takes a much larger amount of energy.
A large jump between the second and third ionization energies suggests two valence electrons for a main-group atom.

ELECTRON AFFINITY
Use the course’s energy-change convention: A gaseous atom gains an electron to form a gaseous negative ion.
Negative electron affinity means energy is released.
More negative means more energy released.
Positive electron affinity means energy is required.
Do not confuse “more negative electron affinity” with “larger positive ionization energy.”
Ionization energy removes an electron. Electron affinity describes adding one.

METALLIC CHARACTER
Metals tend to lose electrons.
Metallic character generally increases down and to the left.
Nonmetallic character generally increases up and to the right.
