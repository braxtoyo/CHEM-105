CATEGORY 10: MEASUREMENT AND BASIC MATTER

COMMON CONVERSION TRAPS
1 nm = 1E−9 m. 1 MHz = 1E6 Hz. 1 ms = 1E−3 s. 1 g = 1E−3 kg. 1 cm3 = 1 mL. 1 L = 1000 mL.
Hz means per second. ms means milliseconds.
Cancel unwanted units using conversion factors.
Square conversion factors for area. Cube conversion factors for volume.
Use density to connect mass and volume.
Kelvin temperature is Celsius temperature plus 273.15.

SCIENTIFIC NOTATION
Scientific notation uses a coefficient with magnitude at least 1 and less than 10, multiplied by a power of ten.
Multiplication: multiply coefficients and add exponents.
Division: divide coefficients and subtract exponents.
A negative exponent indicates a small magnitude, not a negative value.
Calculator E notation represents a power of ten.

SIGNIFICANT FIGURES
Nonzero digits count.
Zeros between nonzero digits count.
Leading zeros do not count.
Trailing zeros after a decimal point count.
Trailing zeros in whole numbers without a decimal can be ambiguous. Scientific notation clarifies precision.
Examples: 0.00450 has 3 significant figures. 4.502 has 4. 3.900 has 4. 620. has 3. 6.20E2 has 3.

ROUNDING RULES
Multiply or divide: answer has the fewest significant figures among measured inputs.
Add or subtract: answer ends at the least precise decimal place.
Exact counts and defined conversion factors do not limit precision.
Keep extra digits during calculations. Round at the end.
A measured value contains certain digits plus one estimated digit.

METRIC PREFIXES
Each entry gives the number of base units in one prefixed unit.
exa, E: 10^18. peta, P: 10^15. tera, T: 10^12. giga, G: 10^9. mega, M: 10^6. kilo, k: 10^3. hecto, h: 10^2. deka, da: 10^1. base unit: 10^0. deci, d: 10^−1. centi, c: 10^−2. milli, m: 10^−3. micro, μ: 10^−6. nano, n: 10^−9. pico, p: 10^−12. femto, f: 10^−15. atto, a: 10^−18.
Capitalization matters: M is mega; m is milli.

MATTER CLASSES
Matter has mass and occupies space.
Element: one type of atom.
Compound: different elements chemically combined in fixed proportions.
Molecule: two or more chemically bonded atoms.
Not every compound consists of separate molecules.
Pure substance: element or compound with fixed composition.
Mixture: substances physically combined in variable proportions.
Homogeneous mixture: uniform throughout.
Heterogeneous mixture: not uniform throughout.
Mixtures can be separated physically. Separating a compound into its elements requires chemical change.

PROPERTIES AND CHANGES
Physical property: observed without changing chemical identity. Examples: density, color, melting point, conductivity.
Chemical property: ability to undergo chemical change. Examples: flammability, reaction with acid.
Physical change: chemical identity stays the same. Examples: melting, boiling, cutting.
Chemical change: new substances form. Examples: burning, rusting.
Intensive property: independent of sample amount. Examples: density, temperature.
Extensive property: depends on sample amount. Examples: mass, volume, total energy.

STATES OF MATTER
Solid: fixed shape and volume.
Liquid: fixed volume; takes container shape.
Gas: takes container shape and volume.

PHASE CHANGES
Melting: solid to liquid. Freezing: liquid to solid. Vaporization: liquid to gas. Condensation: gas to liquid. Sublimation: solid to gas. Deposition: gas to solid.
Melting, vaporization, and sublimation absorb energy.
Freezing, condensation, and deposition release energy.
Phase changes are physical changes.

ENERGY
Kinetic energy: energy of motion.
Potential energy: energy associated with position or arrangement.
Radiant energy: energy carried by electromagnetic radiation.
