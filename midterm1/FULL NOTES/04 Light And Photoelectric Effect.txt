CATEGORY 4: LIGHT AND PHOTOELECTRIC EFFECT

LIGHT VOCABULARY
λ means wavelength: distance between corresponding points on neighboring waves.
ν means frequency: cycles per second.
Frequency units are Hz, meaning per second.
v means particle speed, not frequency.
Amplitude is wave height.
A photon is a discrete packet of electromagnetic energy.

ELECTROMAGNETIC ORDER
From lowest frequency and photon energy to highest:
Radio. Microwave. Infrared. Visible. Ultraviolet. X-ray. Gamma.
Wavelength decreases in that order.
The course guide says to memorize the order, not numerical boundaries.

VISIBLE COLORS
From lowest photon energy to highest: Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet.
Red has the longest visible wavelength and lowest visible frequency and photon energy.
Violet has the shortest visible wavelength and highest visible frequency and photon energy.
Infrared is beyond red at longer wavelengths.
Ultraviolet is beyond violet at shorter wavelengths.

RELATIONSHIPS
Longer wavelength means lower frequency and photon energy.
Shorter wavelength means higher frequency and photon energy.
At fixed frequency, higher intensity does not increase energy per photon.
For monochromatic light, higher intensity means more photons arriving per unit area per unit time.
Use the wavelength-frequency and photon-energy equations on the equation sheet.

BLACKBODY RADIATION
Heated objects emit electromagnetic radiation.
As temperature rises, peak emission shifts toward shorter wavelengths.
Planck explained this behavior using quantized energy exchanges.

PHOTOELECTRIC THRESHOLD
Φ is the work function: minimum energy needed to remove an electron from the material.
Photon energy below Φ: no electron is emitted in the ordinary single-photon process.
Photon energy equal to Φ: threshold; maximum emitted kinetic energy is zero.
Photon energy above Φ: the extra energy becomes electron kinetic energy.
Threshold frequency is the minimum frequency that can produce emission.
Threshold wavelength is the maximum wavelength that can produce emission.
Higher frequency means shorter wavelength.

FREQUENCY VERSUS INTENSITY
Increasing frequency above threshold increases maximum electron kinetic energy.
Increasing intensity at fixed frequency above threshold increases the number of emitted electrons, not their maximum kinetic energy.
Below threshold, increasing intensity does not produce ordinary single-photon emission.

CALCULATION CLUES
For emitted electron speed: Find photon energy. Subtract the work function to find maximum kinetic energy. Use the kinetic-energy equation to solve for speed.
A negative calculated kinetic energy means the photon is below threshold; no emission occurs.
Use meters for wavelength. Use Hz for frequency. Use joules for energy. Use kilograms for mass.
If energy is given in electron volts, convert to joules before using SI equations.
Use constants provided on the equation sheet. If absent, the electron-volt conversion is 1 eV = 1.602E−19 J.
