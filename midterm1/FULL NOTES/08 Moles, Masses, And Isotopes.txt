CATEGORY 8: MOLES, MASSES, AND ISOTOPES

THREE MASS TERMS
Mass number: whole-number count of protons plus neutrons.
Atomic mass: isotope mass or isotope-weighted average, depending on context.
Periodic-table atomic mass: average over naturally occurring isotopes.
Molar mass: mass per mole, in grams per mole.
An element’s atomic mass in amu and molar mass in grams per mole have numerically corresponding values at this course’s precision.
One amu is one-twelfth the mass of a carbon-12 atom.

ISOTOPE AVERAGES
Multiply each isotope’s mass by its fractional abundance, then add.
Convert percent abundance to a decimal first.
All fractional abundances sum to 1. All percentages sum to 100%.
The average lies between the isotope masses.
For two isotopes, if one fraction is x, the other is 1−x.
A more abundant isotope pulls the average closer to its mass.

MASS SPECTROMETRY
Mass spectrometry separates ions according to mass-to-charge ratio.
It helps determine isotope masses and relative abundances.
Do not describe it as measuring mass alone without considering charge.

MOLES
A mole is a particle-counting unit, like a very large dozen.
One mole contains approximately 6.022E23 specified particles.
Specify whether the particles are atoms, molecules, or formula units.
Use the Avogadro constant supplied on the exam.

CONVERSION WORKFLOW
Grams to moles: divide by molar mass.
Moles to grams: multiply by molar mass.
Moles to particles: multiply by Avogadro’s constant.
Particles to moles: divide by Avogadro’s constant.
For grams to particles, go through moles.
For compound molar mass, multiply each element’s atomic mass by its subscript, then add.

COUNTING ATOMS IN COMPOUNDS
One mole of H2O molecules contains: Two moles of hydrogen atoms. One mole of oxygen atoms. Three moles of atoms total.
Subscripts count atoms per molecule; they do not change the number of molecules.
Use parentheses correctly when a subscript multiplies an entire group.

MASS PERCENT
Mass percent means component mass divided by total mass, times 100.
For an element in a compound, compare that element’s contribution to the compound’s molar mass with the total molar mass.
Mass percent is not the same as atom-count percent.
