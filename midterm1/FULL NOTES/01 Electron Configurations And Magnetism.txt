CATEGORY 1: ELECTRON CONFIGURATIONS AND MAGNETISM

CONFIGURATION EXCEPTIONS
Exceptions specifically named in the study guide:
Cr: [Ar]4s1 3d5. Mo: [Kr]5s1 4d5. Cu: [Ar]4s1 3d10. Ag: [Kr]5s1 4d10. Au: [Xe]6s1 4f14 5d10.
Cr and Mo have half-filled d subshells. Cu, Ag, and Au have filled d subshells.
Nearby s and d energies make these arrangements favorable.
“Half-filled or filled subshell stability” is a useful simplified explanation. Do not apply it as a universal rule that predicts every transition-metal configuration.

TRANSITION-METAL IONS
When forming these cations, remove outer ns electrons before electrons in the (n−1)d subshell.
For the first transition series, remove 4s before 3d.
Filling 4s before 3d does not mean removing 3d first.
Examples: Co: [Ar]4s2 3d7. Co2+: [Ar]3d7.
Fe: [Ar]4s2 3d6. Fe2+: [Ar]3d6. Fe3+: [Ar]3d5.
Cu: [Ar]4s1 3d10. Cu+: [Ar]3d10. Cu2+: [Ar]3d9.
Do not change an ion back to the neutral atom’s electron count when checking its magnetism.

THREE FILLING RULES
Aufbau: fill the lowest available energy orbitals first.
Pauli: each orbital holds at most two electrons, with opposite spins. No two electrons in an atom can have all four quantum numbers identical.
Hund: fill equal-energy orbitals singly with parallel spins before pairing.
For three p orbitals, place one electron in each before adding a second electron to any orbital.

FILLING ORDER
Common filling sequence: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, 5s, 4d, 5p, 6s, 4f, 5d, 6p, 7s, 5f, 6d, 7p.
Capacities: s holds 2 electrons. p holds 6. d holds 10. f holds 14.
This sequence is a basic filling guide. Actual configurations have exceptions, and orbital energies vary with the atom or ion.

COUNT ELECTRONS FIRST
Neutral atom: electron count equals atomic number.
Positive ion: subtract electrons equal to the positive charge.
Negative ion: add electrons equal to the charge magnitude.
Configuration exponents must add to the correct electron count.
Noble-gas shorthand uses the preceding noble gas.
Example: Br has 35 electrons. Br: [Ar]4s2 3d10 4p5. Total: 18+2+10+5 = 35.
The same configuration may be written with 3d before 4s. Written order does not change occupancy.

VALENCE ELECTRONS
For main-group atoms, valence electrons occupy the outermost shell.
Group 1: 1 valence electron. Group 2: 2. Groups 13–18: 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8. Helium: 2.
Transition-metal chemistry can involve outer s and incomplete d subshells.
The course guide excludes filled d and f subshells from its valence-electron counting convention.

MAGNETISM
Diamagnetic: all electrons paired; weakly repelled by a magnetic field.
Paramagnetic: at least one unpaired electron; attracted by a magnetic field.
Check the actual configuration of the atom or ion.
Unpaired-electron counts using Hund filling for isolated atoms or ions:
s1: 1. s2: 0.
p1, p2, p3, p4, p5, p6: 1, 2, 3, 2, 1, 0.
d1, d2, d3, d4, d5, d6, d7, d8, d9, d10: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, 0.
Cu+ has d10: diamagnetic. Cu2+ has d9: paramagnetic.
A filled subshell has no unpaired electrons.
Ferromagnetism involves collective alignment of magnetic moments in certain materials. The study guide gives this lower priority.

LAST-ELECTRON QUANTUM NUMBERS
Identify the last occupied subshell.
Read n from the subshell number. Convert s, p, d, f to l = 0, 1, 2, 3. Draw the orbital boxes. Apply Hund and Pauli. Assign ml and ms using the class’s stated box order and spin convention.
An up arrow is commonly assigned ms = +1/2, but use the stated convention.
Without a specified convention, equivalent degenerate orbitals can allow different ml labels. Do not claim a unique box assignment when the question has not specified one.
